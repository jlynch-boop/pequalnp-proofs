The next column-variation case for L-gct-210

Suppose a nine-dimensional level space L has a three-dimensional
subspace of directions supported in a fixed column. Its column-only
subspace is contained in the sum-zero space. Put the column last,
write that subspace W={1,v}-perp inside C^5 with v nonzero and
sum(v)=0, and let P be the projection to the other four columns.
Then dim P=6. Write r for the last-column direction, s=sum(r), and
t=v dot r. Both s and t descend to linear forms on P because they
annihilate W.

Cofactor and row-sum equations

The five cofactors of the last column, viewed as a vector F(D), are
orthogonal to W. Write F(D)=lambda(D) 1+mu(D) v. The permanent gives

  (5+s) lambda + t mu = 120.

If s were nonzero on ker t, one could scale a vector there to s=-5,
contradicting this equation. Thus s is a scalar multiple of t and
lambda=24 on ker t. In the case t=0 identically this says s=0 and
lambda=24 everywhere.

Let rho be the five-vector of row sums of D. The degree-one
cofactor vector is 6 (sum(rho) 1 - rho). It lies in span(1,v), so
rho lies there as well. Since sum(rho)=-s, the simultaneous
conditions rho=0 and t=0 impose at most two linear constraints.
They cut out a space Q of dimension at least four with row-centered
D and lambda=24.

The quadratic pencil on Q

Denote the rows of D by d_i, each in the centered three-dimensional
space U, and put S=sum_i d_i. The sum of all cofactor quadratic
coefficients is zero, since lambda=24. Expanding the quadratic
matching sums then gives 2 d_i dot (S-d_i)=mu_2 v_i, where mu_2
is the homogeneous degree-two term of mu. Hence the five forms

  norm(2 d_i-S)^2 = A + v_i B,
  A = S dot S, B = -2 mu_2

all have rank at most three. Norm squares and dots are bilinear.
If the five v_i are distinct, every 4 by 4 minor of A+zB vanishes
at five distinct values of z and has degree at most four. Thus
the entire pencil has rank at most three.

An exact reduction when S is surjective and the v_i are distinct

In this case A has rank three. Let R be the common radical of
A and B, and J_i be the map 2 d_i-S into U. If the Gram form
J_i^T J_i=A+v_i B has rank at least two, the dot product restricted
to the image of J_i is nondegenerate. Otherwise a degenerate
two-plane in U would have rank one, and a line has rank at most one.
It follows that J_i kills R at every rank-at-least-two value.
There is at most one other value among the v_i. Two distinct
forms of rank at most one would span the pencil and force its
generic rank at most two. Also S kills R since it is surjective.
The identity sum_i J_i=-3 S makes even the remaining J_i kill R.
Hence every row kills R and R=0.

Choose coordinates with A=diag(I_3,0) and write the block form
of B as [[M,C],[C^T,N]]. Over C(z) the Schur complement must vanish
because the pencil has rank three. It is

  z N - z^2 C^T (I_3+z M)^-1 C.

Its first two coefficients give N=0 and C^T C=0. The image of
C in U is totally isotropic, so rank C is at most one. The absence
of a common radical makes C injective on the nullspace of A.
Thus dim Q<=4 and therefore dim Q=4. The remaining case has an
actual singular pencil with a moving radical, not a fixed radical.

A concrete realization that fails the cubic test

For a centered nonzero null vector n, let row 0 be an arbitrary
a in U and put the remaining rows equal to v_i Z n, with v_0=0
and the four other v_i distinct and nonzero. This four-dimensional
example satisfies the quadratic pencil identity: S=a, the row-0
norm is a dot a, and the other norms are a dot a-4v_i Z(a dot n).
The resulting pencil has a moving radical for nonzero n.

The coefficient linear in a and quadratic in Z of the cofactor
deleting row i>0 is 2 e_2(v_except_i) sum_j a_j n_j^2. The factor
sum_j a_j n_j^2 is a nonzero linear form on U. Otherwise all n_j^2
would be equal, then sum(n_j^2)=0 would give n=0. Since lambda=24,
the four coefficients e_2(v_except_i) must be proportional to v_i.
But sum(v)=0 gives e_2(v_except_i)=e_2(v)+v_i^2. Four distinct
v_i cannot all solve the same monic quadratic. Thus this realization
fails. Other isometric realizations and repeated v_i remain open.

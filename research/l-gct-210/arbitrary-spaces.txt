Necessary conditions for an arbitrary counterexample

Let H be the 24-dimensional complex space of 5 by 5 matrices with
total sum zero. A level space is contained in H and is totally
isotropic for the symmetric bilinear form

  B(U,V) = U dot V - rowSum(U) dot rowSum(V) - colSum(U) dot colSum(V).

Dots are bilinear, with no complex conjugation. This follows by
expanding the degree-one and degree-two coefficients. On H the
operator for B has eigenvalue 1 on the doubly centered directions
and -4 on the row-constant and column-constant directions. It is
nondegenerate. The inverse for entrywise pairing is

  Q^-1(U) = U - (rowSum(U) 1^T + 1 colSum(U)^T) / 4.

Any 9-dimensional example has every row and column projection rank
between two and four. Rank at most one would leave a kernel of
dimension at least eight supported on four rows or columns,
contradicting the checked 4 by 5 bound L-gct-215. Rank five would
allow a direction whose row or column equals the all-ones vector,
which could be canceled to make the permanent zero.

A rank test on annihilating forms

More generally take any row/column rectangle with |R|+|C|=6. Its
coordinate image in a level space cannot contain the all-ones
vector. A direction that makes those entries zero at J would
leave the rows R only |R|-1 available columns. Thus each of the
210 rectangles has a linear annihilating form supported on it
whose coefficients sum to one. This includes the individual row
and column forms.

For each such coefficient matrix M put N(M) = Q^-1(M-J/25). Then
B(T,N(M)) = T dot M for T in H. For the span W of any selection of
these normals, the level space is contained in W-perp. If r is the
rank of the Gram matrix of the chosen normals under B, the radical
of W-perp has dimension dim W - r. The dimension of a totally
isotropic space inside W-perp is at most floor((24-r)/2). Thus a
9-space forces r <= 6 for every choice of these normals.

For row weights alpha_i and column weights beta_j, each with
sum one, the Gram entries can be evaluated without constructing
the normals. The row-row entry (i,k) is

  delta_ik (alpha_i dot alpha_k - 1/4) - (alpha_i dot alpha_k)/4 + 3/50,

and the column-column entries have the same form. The row-column
entry (i,j) is

  alpha_ij beta_ij - (alpha_ij + beta_ij)/4 + 3/50.

These constraints have a definite limitation. Fix the diagonal of
the direction to be zero. Every rectangle with |R|+|C|=6 meets the
diagonal, so all the rectangle conditions hold in this 20-dimensional
space. Its intersection with H has dimension 19. Every complex
quadratic form in 19 variables admits a totally isotropic space of
dimension at least nine. Therefore the first two coefficients and
the rectangle obstruction together cannot exclude all nine-spaces.
This example is about those necessary conditions; higher permanent
coefficients still need to be imposed.

The bound in a 15-dimensional family without a fixed zero block

Consider T=[[A,b],[a^T,0]], where the 4 by 4 core A is doubly centered
and a,b lie in the three-dimensional zero-sum space. This gives
dimension 15. The quadratic condition is sum_ij A_ij^2=0 because
the row and column terms cancel the border squares. Its restriction
to the core variables is nondegenerate of rank nine. If L had
dimension nine in this family, its core image would have dimension
r<=4 and its border kernel W would have dimension w=9-r>=5.

Fix a lift with core A. In the cubic matching sum of the lift plus
z(a0,b0), with (a0,b0) in W, the coefficient of z^2 is b0^T A a0.
Indeed, the third entry of a matching must be in the core outside
the row of b0 and column of a0. The sum over such entries is the
corresponding entry of A, by double centering. Thus the quadratic
form b0^T A a0 vanishes on W. It has rank 2 rank(A), giving
w <= 6-rank(A). Since w>=5, every core matrix has rank at most one.

A linear space of matrices all of rank at most one has either
a common left factor or a common right factor. To see this, take
one nonzero rank-one matrix uv^T. A second rank-one matrix must
share the left or right factor, since otherwise their sum has
rank two. A left-sharing matrix and a right-sharing matrix with
neither factor shared with each other also have rank-two sum.
In the 3 by 3 core representation this gives r<=3. Then w>=6
and the same inequality forces rank(A)=0 for each A, which is
impossible when w<=6 and r+w=9. Hence dim L<=8 in this family.

Exact search over coordinate Fourier spans

The script search_fourier_spans.py considers outer products of
Fourier vectors on each cycle of a permutation of five rows and
each cycle of a permutation of five columns. It tests every
coordinate span of those modes through branch and bound across
all 28 partition pairs up to transposition.

All roots can be taken as powers of the same primitive 60th root.
Sending that root to a primitive 60th root modulo 61 is a ring
homomorphism. Each rejected support has an explicit combination
of coefficients in {1,...,5} where the permanent is unequal to
120 modulo 61. Such a failure also excludes the corresponding
complex span. The coefficient probes exhaust the degree-five
integer simplex on a fixed basis. Evaluation on this simplex
determines a polynomial of degree at most five because its
matrix in the falling-factorial basis is triangular and the
factorials through 5 are invertible modulo 61. This explains
why testing supports of size at most five suffices for the
modular computation.

No coordinate span of dimension nine survives. The largest
passing dimension modulo 61 was six over the 28 partition
pairs. A modular pass alone would supply a candidate to check
over the complex numbers. These exclusions concern spans
of subsets of the specified Fourier bases, including choices
with nonreal modes, and do not establish the arbitrary bound.

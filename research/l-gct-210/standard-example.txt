Partial results for L-gct-210

The standard dimension-seven example is maximal by inclusion.

The desk asks whether every complex linear space L satisfying
perm(J + t) = 120 for all t in L has dimension at most eight, where J
is the 5 by 5 all-ones matrix. The argument below rules out extensions
of the checked L-gct-080 construction. Other subspaces remain open.

Proof for general n >= 3. Put m = n - 1. The standard space W consists
of matrices with zero m by m core, bottom row a with sum zero away
from the corner, right column b arbitrary away from the corner, and
corner -sum(b). It has dimension 2n - 3. The permanent on J + W is n!.

Suppose A is an additional direction and W + C A is a level space.
Write A's core as X, bottom row as r, right column as q, and corner
as s. Write B(z) = J_m + z X, P(z) = perm(B(z)), and P_ij(z) for
the permanental minor of B(z) deleting row i and column j. Splitting
permutations according to the final row and column gives

  perm(J + W(a,b) + z A)
    = (1 + zs - sum(b)) P(z)
      + sum_{i,j} (1 + z q_i + b_i)(1 + z r_j + a_j) P_ij(z).

1. The mixed a,b term vanishes for every a with sum zero. For each
   i, P_ij(z) is therefore independent of j. The coefficient of z
   in P_ij(z) is (m-2)! times the sum of X entries outside row i and
   column j. If C_j is column j's sum, X_ij - C_j is independent of
   j for each i. Summing this relation over i shows that (1-m) C_j
   is independent of j. Since m >= 2, all C_j are equal, so each row
   of X is constant. Call its value x_i.

2. Set a=0 and compare coefficients of b_i. Since each row of B(z)
   is constant, write R = sum_j r_j and obtain

   [b_i] perm(J + W(0,b) + z A)
     = (m-1)! z product_{h != i}(1 + z x_h) (R - m x_i).

   This polynomial vanishes. The product has constant term one,
   so every x_i = R/m. Denote the common value by x.

3. Subtract the element of W with bottom row r - x 1 and right
   column q from A. The resulting direction A' has all first m
   columns constantly x, upper-right entries zero, and corner
   t = s + sum(q). Along z A' the permanent becomes

   perm(J + z A') = m! (1 + z x)^m (n + z t) = n!.

   The linear coefficient gives t = -n m x. After substitution the
   quadratic coefficient divided by m! is -n m (m+1) x^2 / 2.
   Thus x = t = 0, A' = 0, and A belonged to W already.

Exact check at n=5. Each allowed extension direction must be in
the kernel of every gradient row D perm(J + W(a,b)). The script
verify_star.py computes those rows at 20 integer sample points, using
the zero vector and each standard basis vector in the three free
row parameters and the four free column parameters. Each gradient
entry is constant plus terms linear in either parameter group or
bilinear between them, so these evaluated rows span all the gradients.

Exact rational elimination gives rank 17, hence an 8-dimensional
common tangent kernel. The extra direction modulo W is the matrix
with all first four columns equal to one, upper-right entries zero,
and corner -20. Its permanent polynomial is

  perm(J + z A') = 120 (1+z)^4 (1-4z),

whose quadratic coefficient is -1200. The script independently
checks the gradient rank, the tangent basis, and this polynomial.

A dimension bound for a fixed zero 3 by 3 block

Write a direction as T = [[0,C],[D,E]], with top-left block 3 by 3.
Every four-edge matching with nonzero product uses two entries of C
and two of D. It uses neither E nor the zero block. Thus the degree
four coefficient of perm(J + z T) is Q(C) Q(D), where Q is the sum of
all 2 by 2 subpermanents, with D transposed if needed.

For a linear level space L this product is identically zero. The
polynomial ring on L has no zero divisors, so one of the factors
vanishes identically. On a 3 by 2 matrix with columns a,b, the form
Q is a^T (J_3 - I_3) b. The middle matrix is invertible, so this is
a nondegenerate quadratic form on six coordinates. A totally
isotropic projection has dimension at most three.

The kernel of that projection lives on two rows or two columns.
The linear and quadratic coefficients impose total sum zero and
Q = 0. On two columns x,y, writing X = sum(x) and Y = sum(y), this
quadratic form is X Y - x^T y. It is nondegenerate on the 9-dimensional
hyperplane X+Y=0. For example, decompose each column into a constant
and a zero-sum vector. On the scalar coordinate X the restricted
form is -4 X^2/5, and on the two zero-sum vectors it is their negative
dot product. The kernel thus has dimension at most four. Rank-nullity
gives dim L <= 7 for the whole fixed-zero-block family.

A restriction on a fixed zero 2 by 3 block

Now use the same block notation with C of size 2 by 2 and D of size
3 by 3. The degree five coefficient is perm(C) perm(D). If perm(C)
vanishes identically, its nondegenerate quadratic form bounds the
C-projection by two. The kernel is supported on three rows, and
the checked L-gct-156 bounds it by six for five columns. Such an L
has dimension at most eight.

If dim L >= 9, perm(D) must therefore vanish identically. Its
projection has dimension at least five because the kernel is
supported on two columns and has dimension at most four. Chan and
Ilten classify the six-dimensional vector subspaces of the 3 by 3
permanental hypersurface as the six spaces with one fixed zero row
or column. A projection cannot have higher dimension, since that
would give infinitely many six-dimensional subspaces. A fixed
zero row in D gives a 3 by 3 zero block in T; a fixed zero column
gives a zero column in T, reducing to the checked 4 by 5 bound
L-gct-215. Both cases give dimension at most seven.

Consequently in this supported family a candidate of dimension
at least nine must have a D-projection of dimension exactly five
and kernel dimension four. Chan and Ilten's section 7B describes
the five-dimensional projections, using projective dimension four.
This is the initial reduction. The subsequent proof in
zero-block-bound.txt handles the remaining projections and gives
the bound of eight for the fixed zero 2 by 3 block.

Source for the projection classification
Melody Chan and Nathan Ilten, Fano schemes of determinants and
permanents, Algebra & Number Theory 9 (2015), section 7B.
https://msp.org/ant/2015/9-3/ant-v9-n3-p05-s.pdf

These partial results are mathematical derivations, with an exact
arithmetic cross-check for the standard example. The global complex
bound L-gct-210 remains unresolved.
